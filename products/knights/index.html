Knights — The Last Move (https://alexscalancom.github.io/knights.app)**

A quiet, single-player strategy game you play against the computer. It runs entirely in your browser: no account, no sign-in, no server, no ads, no tracking.

How to win: both players draw one shared path across the board. The player who makes the last legal move wins.

The idea in one paragraph

Knights is not chess. There are no pieces to capture and no board to defend. White and Black take turns placing knights onto one single path. Every cell you use is used forever — it becomes blocked for both players. Because the path can never branch and can never be reused, the game always ends: whoever has a legal move left at the very end is the winner. It is a short, purely logical puzzle — usually 2–10 minutes per game, and always with a clear result.

The rules

  1. White always moves first. Before a game starts you choose whether you play White (you place the first knight) or Black (the computer does).
  2. Your first move may be placed on any empty playable cell. There is no knight restriction on the opening move.
  3. Every later move must be a knight move — the chess "L" jump of two cells in one direction and one cell in the other — measured from the last cell placed on the path, no matter which player placed it.
  4. A cell can never be visited twice. Visited cells stay blocked for the rest of the game and are drawn as filled squares.
  5. Knights jump over everything. Walls, visited cells and cells outside the board shape may sit between the two cells; only the destination has to be valid.
  6. No legal move means you lose. After each move the remaining legal moves are recalculated; the player who just moved wins when nothing is left.

There is no draw and no timeout. A game always produces exactly one winner.

During a game

The game screen keeps everything you need in one place: whose turn it is, the move number, the elapsed time, the current board and opponent, and these controls:

  • Pause / Resume — the clock stops and the computer waits for you.
  • Undo — steps back to the position where it is your turn again.
  • Resign — ends the game and records it as abandoned.
  • New game — starts over with the same or a different setup.

The timer counts only real playing time. Paused time, closed tabs, and idle time are not counted, so the recorded duration of a game is honest even if you left in the middle.

An unfinished game is never lost

The game is saved after every move. If you close the tab, lose connection, or reload the page, the app offers to resume the game: the board, the move history, whose turn it is, the elapsed time, and the settings are all restored. If the computer was thinking when the page closed, it simply starts thinking again from the restored position.

Statistics, history, and medals

Everything is available from the Stats, History, and Medals screens.

Statistics (separate views for all games, Classic, and Challenge):

  • games played, wins, losses, win rate, abandoned games
  • best winning streak
  • average game length, average thinking time, average number of moves
  • longest game, total time spent thinking
  • breakdowns by board size, by opponent strength, by board shape, and by level

History keeps the last 30 completed games per mode, with filters by mode and result, showing the board or level played, the opponent strength, the result, the number of moves, the duration, and how long you spent thinking. Older entries are dropped automatically — your lifetime totals, medals, and level progress are never affected.

Your data stays on your device

Games, statistics, medals, level progress, and settings are stored only in this browser (offline local storage). Nothing is uploaded, nothing is synced, and there is no account to create.

From Settings you can:

  • Export — download everything as a single JSON file you can keep or move to another device.
  • Import — restore such a file.
  • Delete all data — erase every game, statistic, and medal on this device, after a confirmation.

Clearing your browser data or uninstalling the app removes your progress, so export it first if you want to keep it.

Install it and play offline

Knights is an installable app (a Progressive Web App). After the first visit it works with no connection at all — on a plane, in a tunnel, or with the Wi‑Fi off.

  • Android / Chrome: open the site, then Add to home screen or Install app.
  • iPhone / iPad / Safari: tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.
  • Desktop Chrome or Edge: use the install icon in the address bar.

Installing gives the game its own icon, its own window, and full offline play. Updates are applied automatically the next time you are online.

Settings

  • Theme: light, dark, or follow your device.
  • Sound effects and animations: on or off.
  • Defaults: classic board size, opponent strength, and your colour, so the next game starts the way you like it.
  • Your data: export, import, delete.

A few tips for your first games

  • The board is not the resource — the move count is. You win by being the last one who can still move, not by covering the board.
  • Early moves decide the shape of the whole path. Try to leave yourself exactly one good reply rather than many tempting ones.
  • Corners and narrow shapes are strong: a knight in a corner has very few exits, and a path that ends in a corner usually ends the game in your favour.
  • Playing Black is not a handicap. Moving second means you see the opening and answer it — on many levels it is the stronger seat.
  • If you lose a level, play it again. The layout never changes, so every attempt teaches you something real about it.

Requirements

  • Any current desktop or mobile browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, Safari).
  • A mouse, a keyboard, or a touch screen — the board is played by tapping cells.
  • JavaScript must be enabled.
  • No internet connection is needed after the first visit.
  • The layout adapts to phones, tablets, and desktops, including the 16 × 16 board.

About this repository

This repository contains the built, ready-to-serve application: the HTML page, the compiled scripts and styles, the level data, and the offline service worker. You can host these files on any static web host or open them from a subdirectory — no backend, no database server, and no build step are required to run the game.

The game itself is a Progressive Web App built with Svelte 5, TypeScript, and Vite. The level definitions in levels/levels.json are fixed data, so the 50 challenge levels are the same for every player.

Your progress lives in your browser, so a new device always starts from zero games and zero medals.
